# src/navigation

Navigators, split views, headers, startup hooks and taps from outside the app. How screens open items and what Today and first launch show is in `src/screens/AGENTS.md`.

- `App.tsx` only sets up the providers and picks login or main UI. The navigators are in `src/navigation/` (`stacks.tsx` for the tab and modal stacks, `DetailStack.tsx` for the split view's detail pane, `MainTab.tsx` with its `tabs` table, and `index.tsx` for the root), with shared header options in `navigation/options.tsx`. Split views: `NativeSplitView.tsx` (UISplitViewController, one per tab on iPad and iPhone), `SidebarSplitNavigator.tsx` (the Mac's sidebar of tabs) and `SplitView.tsx` (Android's, drawn in JS, which holds the whole main UI and keeps a detail stack per tab, shown while its tab is selected (`SplitTab`), plus the `SplitViewContext` all three provide). Every tab has a detail pane of its own on every platform. A stack lists the item detail screens it shows with `renderItemDetails([...])` and the settings screens with `renderSettingDetails()`; its param list is built from `ItemDetailParams` and `SettingDetailParams` in `navigation/types.ts` to match. The colors and the navigation theme are in `src/theme/`. Startup work lives in hooks: `useStoresLoaded`, `useReLogin`, `useShareHandler` and `useMainStartup` (semester, courses, timetable, background sync, server actions and taps from outside the app once logged in). Android's update check is `hooks/useAvailableUpdate` (the TUNA mirror, compared with the installed app's native version, not `package.json`, which an over-the-air update can change).
- Taps from outside the app (notifications, widget rows and other `learnx://` links, Spotlight results) are opened by `useNotificationTaps`, `useLinkTaps` and `useSpotlightTaps` (started by `useMainStartup`) through `useOpenTarget`: it shows the item's list and sets the pending item (`setPendingTarget`), which the list opens with `usePendingTarget` (in its split view's detail pane when there is one). The target types, their routes (`targetRoutes`) and the `learnx://` links are in `helpers/openTarget`.
- Headers: each tab's first screen has a large title (`tabRootOptions` in `navigation/options.tsx`) on iPhone and iPad from iOS 26, where its subtitle (the filter, the date) is native; below iOS 26 the subtitle is our own `HeaderTitle`, which a large title would hide, so those keep the small title, as does the Mac. A large title collapses with the screen's first scroll view, so put anything above a list in its header (`SyncStatus` in `FilterList`) or a `Form`'s first section (`SyncStatusSection` on Today); a view above it also sits behind the transparent header.
- From iOS 26 on iPhone and iPad every stack's header is transparent and its back button the chevron alone (`stackScreenOptions`), as SwiftUI shows it (on iOS 27 a titled one fades out and in after a push in which header buttons morph, in plain UIKit too), so every screen is laid out under the header and scrolls under its glass: a scroll view keeps clear of the header itself (`contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior="automatic"`, which our `FlatList`, `Form` and the web views have), and a view over the top of the content keeps clear of the header with react-native-screens' `SafeAreaView` top edge (`PageProgressBar`, Course detail's floating tabs). The header blurs what scrolls under it only for the screen's first scroll view, so Course detail shows its selected list alone there, with no pager. The bars' titles, buttons and the status bar follow the app's appearance, not what is under them, so content that stays white in dark mode (a previewed file in File detail) doesn't go under them at all: it keeps clear of the header and the tab bar (`SafeArea`'s `clearOfBars`).
- That blur is the soft scroll edge effect: UIKit's automatic style is the hard one (an opaque band with a line) in an iPad split view's columns other than the first, so `ios/learnX/ScrollEdgeEffect.swift` gives every UIKit scroll view the soft one and `Form` sets it for SwiftUI's (not on Mac). Don't set `headerTransparent` or `headerStyle` per screen (a test checks the stacks). Before iOS 26 the header takes a `Form`'s gray.
