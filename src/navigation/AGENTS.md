# src/navigation

Navigators, split views, headers, startup hooks and taps from outside the app. How screens open items is in `src/screens/AGENTS.md`.

- `App.tsx` only sets up the providers and picks login or main UI. Each platform has its own split view (UIKit's on iPhone and iPad, a sidebar of tabs on the Mac, one drawn in JS on Android and in the Electron app), and all of them provide `SplitViewContext`. Every tab has a detail pane of its own on every platform, and its own full screen: File detail's Full Screen hides that tab's list only.
- A stack lists the item detail screens it shows with `renderItemDetails([...])` and the settings screens with `renderSettingDetails()`; its param list is built from `ItemDetailParams` and `SettingDetailParams` in `navigation/types.ts` to match.
- Startup work lives in hooks (`useMainStartup` and the hooks `App.tsx` calls), not in components. Android's update check compares with the installed app's native version, not `package.json`'s, which an over-the-air update can change.
- Taps from outside the app (notifications, widgets and other `learnx://` links, Spotlight) all go through `useOpenTarget`: it shows the item's list and sets a pending target, which the list opens with `usePendingTarget`, in its split view's detail pane when there is one. The target types, their routes and the `learnx://` links are in `helpers/openTarget`.
- Headers: each tab's first screen has a large title on iPhone and iPad from iOS 26, where its subtitle is native. Below iOS 26 the subtitle is our own `HeaderTitle`, which a large title would hide, so those keep the small title, as does the Mac. A large title collapses with the screen's first scroll view, so put anything above a list in its header or a `Form`'s first section; a view above it would also sit behind the transparent header.
- From iOS 26 on iPhone and iPad every stack's header is transparent, so every screen is laid out under the header and scrolls under its glass. A scroll view keeps clear of the header with `constants/scrollInsetBehavior` (our `FlatList`, the web views, Search and `Form` already do), and a view over the top of the content with react-native-screens' `SafeAreaView` top edge. Neither applies on the Mac, where the header is opaque and the screen is below it. The header blurs only the screen's first scroll view, which is why Course detail has no pager there. The bars follow the app's appearance, not what is under them, so content that stays white in dark mode (a previewed file) keeps clear of the bars entirely (`SafeArea`'s `clearOfBars`).
- That blur is the soft scroll edge effect. UIKit gives an iPad split view's later columns the hard one, so `ios/learnX/ScrollEdgeEffect.swift` makes every UIKit scroll view soft and `Form` sets it for SwiftUI's. Don't set `headerTransparent` or `headerStyle` per screen (a test checks the stacks).
